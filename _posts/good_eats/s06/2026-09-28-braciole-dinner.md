Dinner tonight was a {% good_eats_recipe_link Braciole %} for the main course, along with a salad
plus {% good_eats_recipe_link Thousand Island Dressing %}.

It started in the afternoon with preparing with meat. I began with a 1.75 pound flank steak.

![flank steak](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPyG47Av7eZrR8V13ZnikrN4AEAz-kCfGNk3-O3ML2k0NNw1WaG2NRKoKvYPoBg2Vthzat6Deay-zeAwpOwR3l18Al3bIOzhI_QwYswacZRw_-umkh72upeowt1u0-SjAWxZYoXYfcjuAgFgu7k_ObJ=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I laid it out on top of two sheets of cling wrap, then started the job of pounding it flat with a meat
mallet. This was grueling work. At first it seemed like the meat wasn't moving at all, and I was getting
nowhere. I took a few breaks and came back to it a bunch of times over the course of an hour or so, in
between meetings and stuff. Eventually I got it to where I thought it was workable.

![flattened meat](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMvzEEoElkaFoSBQYFP_ie1pM4kcpLZ3OCkr-yPpaKpUuNO1rllYFgh_d3OIKZrRApsOC9cT22keEVmHQy_cnvV8TiClkb0SFoFi0MSCp6sNHIalkOJufrRX16ObapsOCW8cwsvM3m7JvEr0C-bBtht=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

At some point during the process I googled for tips on flattening a flank steak. Most people on the
internet agreed that the best way is to butterfly it, which of course I was too late for. I think in
the end what helped me was:

* Cutting away some of the silverskin on each side of the meat. I used the tip of a knife to get under
  it and and then pulled it up. Some came right off, but what was left was broken enough to (I think)
  help the meat move
* Letting it come to room temperature. I should have realized this earlier, but the flattening process
  got easier and easier the warmer the steak got

So with a flattened steak, it was time to food-process the filling and spread it over the meat. For this
I used some gluten free seasoned croutons, which are indistinguishable from gluten-full ones.

![filling on the meat](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczN5jvNLEftDBPjWytZ5KHRqZ7-hLlDWTvQCGOUkh0ZdmdaQh2APC6rT68vP8XI9s8Ud5cW-nONw2KEF6EqTFpMkU8k8P0sLRZLCCzk2DsSKs5rEE9QBQO2H_XtNEXtTrvSZzzCQrESdFFMpf0bUl7-r=w2484-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

Then it was time to roll and tie it. That was cool because this is a process I've never done before. It
took a couple tries to get the "looping" right, but after I got the motion down, it was pretty easy.

![tied up meat](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPqYdyLQBoIGo-9ZGAlGeBBe6UNkeiUYD7Ep6XyV2pA4OYtyldXL3NmyZzcHLH6OJm4mDM5IvVCN7M6CvKZECEWDej-einHVunHVqqKGB_ctERTc7xfL7czBWltlys-Jng7bC7YD5ZlZNbFY7SS7nn-=w2754-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

After that I browned all sides of the braciole in a cast iron pan. I needed to bend it a little because
the roll was a bit longer than the 12 inch pan could handle.

![browning](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNcG2VO7ZDpkJoDgpu-3BXxFVhAI4VIAW1AAV11i9iC4AFgo8y5UIAhnM3mor9Mu8nVDWOrLvcx6BfxWipXt2Lf3yON6orNum9WQ2F8KGnuZN-BXqQp3r7BhFTlzsYdw8N3-6Uz2KaUXhKm_QzKw_Ob=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

Once it was browned, I put it into the pan with some store-bought marinara (not tomato sauce) that had been
heating in the oven. I tented the pan with foil and popped it into the oven at 350 degrees. I then turned
my attention to making the salad dressing.

![dressing ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMyNO3Lorzx63XtRRH-uAjVQsGgJrWG4xd_OljewuyMmmkKA64p4rxmZAPBvtuPDI2gDhbfyehnXJpvdmn7p6NTWqchwS51UXCm-3yrsvulhhDs_xzWlzcoLE8ro0_6Ki_R52cEoxEcSsV1kLgY4Dnl=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

This couldn't have been easier, it was just one step: "mix everything together in a bowl". I halved this recipe
because I couldn't see us needing 2 cups of salad dressing any time soon. Once it was all mixed I put it into
a jar for serving.

![jar of dressing](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNUiFUpGsxS_O9I68ztY1VHquoTwRDyCvJ8oOM54LgMVZMkpkxIc8XHlku_c21rq03g-QhdhFi20G4qqx7J0ITAh2yfDx-BlOBZjlwO6rB3rlqd-lqEjGa4Iq6yqz51WKdS1I_NgGQwZ9h2kBUjtn4U=w2614-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

An hour after I put the braciole in the oven, I dropped the temp down to 325 and gave it one more half hour. When
I took the meat out of the oven, I moved it onto a cutting board and let it cool for about 10 minutes before
cutting it.

![cut braciole](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMFN2P6DX2nd3dznswTj0hWOT2bEywL-Rqg0DRuXcLh94AD5_RXwbzRNm4eLt9furfu0t9JGp_SKJSPX9wmbikXsqcXBsSUuvmtv3Z_Ou6D-eEy9OnJ92PcV_EZulkk7LuY8xJYGDyaEpE_IyL14p0z=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I have to say this one was a _hit_. Everyone in the family loved it, and everyone had seconds. The meat was so
tender, and the filling was absolutely delicious. But an unexpected star was the sauce -- having cooked the
sauce for so long resulted in it being thick and caramelized, and just full of concentrated flavor. It paired with
the braciole _so_ well. The salad dressing was alright -- it's a decent low-calorie alternative to normal
thousand island -- bit the beef was a winner.
