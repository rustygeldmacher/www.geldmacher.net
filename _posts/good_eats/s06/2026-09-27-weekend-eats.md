Had a little time at home this weekend, and some guests staying over so I decided I'd try out a few
recipes while we were here. Since {% good_eats_recipe_link Clotted Cream %} takes about 24 hours, I
started there. I was able to find some [non-ultra-pasteurized heavy cream](https://www.highlawnfarm.com/milk-and-cream)
at Whole Foods, so I started there by pouring a pint of it into a coffee filter fitted over a strainer,
which was over a bowl.

![poured cream](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPRBPVx9ojAPz-x9yidy8pTRxJHBYwvMT_KwM4oQVGBfZy__2gSFAv9o2S-howlyS79AoMLiZDO55q_hgKnNYPtzFATmV3td28wN507xH_ia2AN1SsHfsfbn_szoL8crUBobJibnCzZzQJ58O190XJI=w2532-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

From there, I scraped/stirred it every 2 hours for the next 10 hours. Meanwhile, while we were waiting
for guests to arrive I started on the {% good_eats_recipe_link Cheese Souffle %} by gathering up my
ingredients.

![souffle ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczN3CFx2e5drr31O2QZjSSs0GT4eXqhcypdHsEjOPFPdbDyz078NfbTwuLGi4Z3EpNlUOLmBfc42kf2nA9tldfIM3LS1FKPFu7Z3p70foqbKlocm1p0UrpPnbbJCdXdzAPchE_ksgz7EBLOYdJr9ONMt=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

Of course souffles are notoriously scary to make, so I took my time as went slowly and carefully. The first
step was to make the cheese sauce.

![cheese sauce prep](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczO8Yx3--gT_Em39paUW3GFTUQiJt8eLbNVFOFet7E-hm0rMMfVP2YemkuUnuXG_3V2Z4Ulgn7yO90qExGZv2frGxBxaT82nazBTwSngg3JxfdRH2iqeozAFVNjWw_a4heZGw5VMyBXKEFQwGpaBxn8R=w1336-h1780-s-no-gm?w=400)

The sauce came together well. I used a GF measure-for-measure flour for the roux, which worked fine, though
I think the roux was a bit darker than it should have been. Though, cooking the water out of the butter is
a bit tricky to time right -- by the time I thought it had all been cooked out (the bubbles stopped being
large and frequent) I think the butter started to brown. So that's why in some of the following photos you'll
see the cheese sauce looking dark (though no less delicious). When the cheese sauce was done and it was cooling
to room temperature, I started on the eggs whites. In the show, he added a tablespoon of water, which was not
in the online recipe. I used the extra water, though not sure if it made a difference. I then beat the egg
whites to medium peaks.

![medium peaks](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczONW1Ei7rRccUJ6LyaqY5PoxbEn8LvEaqjRiuBFS5gxXp0c6gGAvxTN1a9zAUQ4fruu5NBkBGTC8JaACwW1cjI0O3IqHrBlQmzfn6GBS6Eg2QF01mYHFmP0gO9SKwCM_6ij9cJMkH_6ooShXnT4RfRU=w2110-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I thought I did a pretty good job with that, not knowing what to expect and how long to do beat them for. Then
it was time to fold the foam into the cheese sauce.

![getting ready to fold](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMFYNj_2BWCTE6OLKXLV921qihBeVZrxPSBHhfV-lAkqdq550OE0c2bVSmF_RMGX5-Lh_V6YUMBnpCPoIPe4ueE-3S_SeDwU6MWD8OJlnciCsbu-4PMEMkY8Zv0ADNvds-GGMVkptsyH-NCh_W_qur1=w2660-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

You can see above how the cheese sauce was a bit dark. Maybe next time using white cheddar rather than yellow
would lighten it a bit? Not sure, but I don't think the color affected the final flavor. Folding in the foam
was another place that was scary because I didn't want to break the foam down and lose all the air I had put
into the eggs from beating them. But, it actually turned out fine -- again, just going slowly and carefully
was the name of the game. After folding all the foam in, it was into the pan and into the oven.

![in the pan](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczP6BM2wdJyMmyD1bHWSSob03sEP0dCBYFWVfC_jaJ0_q5DvIUwjZ7x-1KB8tl27PJpKGDOZAh-HMw45beYaOz8tw63F27HU8XFgPPjAA6SiF_apic8-dcCq45egi2pnqTt7uCZRGWYwefpY5C5V4SO5=w2230-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

Next came the cook, and here's where I messed it up a bit. I put the souffle on the middle rack of the oven,
not taking into account the enormous rise that I'd get while it cooked. So, what ended up happening was that
it rose right into and through the top rack of the oven.

![cooked souffle](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPCPcI9QMdobT22LItw3MyeCMSc1UDnFBO9mWppJfHfmiv5BZ3vWJxerhsKgMYl3_oDH7_WC6PGER5BTnP2YWKPRJN9m43MSlF1WiTlXoqnGfmAnylPSLLYOJpX92DJsyJgYw9T383kLmYAyJWe8CIQ=w2702-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I had to kind of scrape it out of there, which was too bad because I feel like the top would have been absolutely
perfect. As it was, though, even with an ugly and half-missing top, the souffle was superb. All of our guests
had a good laugh at the oven mistake while we chowed down on it.

The next morning, it was time to take the clotted cream out of the fridge.

![clotted cream right out of the fridge](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNPjmYocC94k-phgyphWW-Rhfmm_jOYjTktFauXR4CJxMqwSmuuZCCEGaOzAJO2SVSGosexcGUlHU69q_rOEjm8NcfLBO9bLe1x4ho-JF48PDm_S4SkntxjqXz_9JC5ojTM8vGFKG9vng5w52JkMGD_=w1336-h1780-s-no-gm?w=400)

It seemed like all the liquid was drained, so I scraped what remained off the coffee filter and into another
bowl.

![clotted cream in a bowl](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNQjFikderrpzXotUNYuHuHla2r8PnJFlnsm75P9EpljBDgl--qah180f0T0OhfsVBJpD17OZRdAtsVM4bAfUTeoyD_tSDrR1yYSHypP69SyutL8LMDP0XgD-FpReMXCfdHkqdJYQZSdMqB4jgHRfmx=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

From there I mixed small amounts of the liquid into the bowl until it was the consistency of cold cream
cheese. Meanwhile, Gemma baked some blueberry scones, and when those were done we tried the cream on them.
It was delightful -- kind of like a cross between whipped cream and butter. Our guests enjoyed it too.

Side note. I spent a bunch of time reading around and discovered that this clotted cream recipe is not
actually clotted cream. It's closer to butter, but not really that, even, because it's not churned. This
recipe is really just isolated cream fat, which is really good by itself, but not clotted cream as it
traditionally exists. I did notice that Alton has an [updated recipe](https://altonbrown.com/recipes/slow-cooker-clotted-cream/)
on his web site, and I did try that one first, though it didn't work for me -- I think my little 2 quart
crock pot's "warm" setting is not warm enough, because I got only the thinnest little layer of solids at
the top after 10 hours.
