Making {% good_eats_recipe_link Strawberry Pudding %} started with husking a pint of strawberries
and making the {% good_eats_recipe_link Macerated Strawberries %} the night before.

![macerated strawberry ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOwW6a-93eN-5w54V8fC4jsedpE8TLp2uo-nbqnpIkXejy0d2G1tUwicEYuK59Xw8GrsdkgbKGpsPwoxdWfxbxLTs8Dd8wNuIaIqiQ4P5vP_Udyw7fwuKGr2xeqSCO6fAbPKiCvJMcygb5nFEhDk-zd=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

Reading through the online comments on this one, I heard that using a regular red wine like the
recommended merlot would make it too "boozy", so I went with one the commenter's suggestions and
used [Ruby Port](https://www.taylor.pt/us/port-wine/fine-ruby) for the wine. Since I halved the
recipe, I used a cup and a quarter of the port in the mix.

![macerated strawberries](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPAkc0MmA9hyC2mNAz-DShmnJ2nYcO-_tel2hrIVLNGP1yaf8oxah52UUj_SrIN5BBF2vCMYzgSwa9VqQifZaGvpxGU2jPEwHXmvMN_wFPm7WsZUJIrcOS-PnWg_bMqDS2ZYwlev_hFw9UZzn5ScQVm=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

I then put them all in the fridge and waited until the next morning, when I gathered up the
ingredients for the puddings. Now, it's tough to find gluten free potato bread off the shelf, and
I didn't really have time to bake some for this recipe, so instead I used a
[brioche loaf](https://www.promiseglutenfree.us/products/brioche-loaf/) from Promise Gluten Free.

![pudding ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPY1ahTlguYsvOg-NJCyOnBP6OdZneo2Aig8vzMwi1EFm3LMopUUaTGQEaWfaAK79Z5OLSz0ezQD7DpCF49JZmssWJW8hh_wkyZEl9yxyDPcd6iO0RkDedWXaBYHQxwXcWMYsu1FQAyIvVHf4Jy87Mp=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

The puddings came together pretty easily. The most difficult part was getting the bottom off of
the cans -- I had to resort to the can opening on a Swiss Army knife because a regular crank
can opener didn't have anything to grab onto. Once I got the puddings in the cans I pushed some
seltzer cans down into them and popped the whole thing in the fridge.

![puddings in cans with seltzers on them](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNlxxAUVYu4U0Fe7x5vOSNyjRGrTHrd5Qt8mQmICjdKlCCdeLOc9ZV5DpRp31xRhLegZ_Iusqp8YHccLcAyfhaAGfGNZ4MMFoutmSKhWq3OeTR9Asb5f0ql7f02vordIYusOwc6NK4ojR1Kn_WjdDIZ=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

About 8 hours later, it was after dinner and time to try the puddings. I get them out of the cans,
I simply lifted the can while pushing on the seltzer.

![plated puddings](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPFsr38MdJoCNUlFO7Bk_l_08FdCfAlAu9VCEhZNVVFzKYsjBJqL3YgbsjLtFUL7WRPLzbUPM885dgWOboHrXrx6NU39zBYBh2W663pNLQl8564vkAOR2eimFfmyjNhVFUUUOme7igm-HT-2oCJwedh=w1356-h1808-s-no-gm?w=400)

The puddings were good! They're _a lot_ of food though. Perhaps one less layer next time? The brioche
was good too, but we think perhaps a biscuit would be better. Definitely don't regret making these,
but would probably not go through the trouble again.

The strawberries, though -- yep, they're boozy. However, the left over "juice" makes for an _incredible_
sipping beverage. We took the left over liquid (with the honey, zest, and sugar) and mixed it half and
half with some of the remaining port and _wow_ it's so good, a perfect dessert drink.
