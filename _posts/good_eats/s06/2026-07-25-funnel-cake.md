We had a free Saturday night, so what better way to celebrate than with some
{% good_eats_recipe_link Funnel Cake %}!

Now, I've made gluten free [pate a choux]({% post_url
good_eats/s06/2026-02-21-season-six-begins %}) already, and that worked out OK
with some modifications. This time around I decided I wanted to see what people
do specifically for GF funnel cake, pate a choux or no. I eventually landed on
[this recipe from the Gluten Free
Austrian](https://theglutenfreeaustrian.com/gluten-free-strauben-austrian-funnel-cake/)
which doesn't make a pate a choux first. So, my rating and experience here will
not be specifically with the Good Eats recipe, but we're going to sometimes have
to do that in order to be gluten free -- that's the tradeoff we're making from
here on. Often, the recipe modifications aren't too drastic, but this time
around they are. But, "making gluten free funnel cake" is higher up my list than
"making the Good Eats funnel cake", otherwise I wouldn't be cooking for my
family.

So with that said, on to the frying!

I started out by making the batter as the site suggested.

![batter](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPyLkqj0VD3Sy237jn0n5IzAmCukZW5-W_bXTf_49MPGayg-Ym6fCm7JxAoR4yisQ_YpdShT_Sv40zl7g_89_k_8Gg-lUkm7TEOa_NADXxftCrY1cTJBLN7Pd4VWQ_7zOfHUo3n31K0F7sa5TrIUQR-=w2242-h1682-s-no-gm?w=500)

You can see from the photo that I have a piping bag in a pitcher. That makes it incredibly
easy to fill the bag without much fuss.

![piping bag](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOhCqEBkYb9EmD744PJP__LUdZGpcXFNW9xMe-XBfdAEjfRd32JX8kqaT4c3GyT8eFosExQaoSE0gexovDl3SqmZoKxNmRP5D_Wfwy_PynP-4CK6gTj1Urob1N27d7IXxzHUha4YyMNZQ72dLvQKDcP=w2674-h1682-s-no-gm?w=500)

Once the batter was made and in the bag, I got the fry oil ready. I used about 3/4 of a gallon
of canola oil and heated it up to 350 degrees. Starting in the middle and going around in
ever larger circles I fried up cakes one by one.

![frying funnel cake](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNeddEIHftnI5AqyQL3-LDjipnvBTQlvDezNBkFIwOQUkRo2QOrpqT54aNVfx1OA8bgMSyPwh34E6qkK1sA9S7Vyix6W2JgDGM04M3NQjzjmJs_-c5UeC3jNXut7EzENSzPKqFdIuwf7XVgfkAvJkBa=w2056-h1682-s-no-gm?w=500)

After I made a few of them -- they got progressively better as I dialed in my technique -- we
dusted them with powdered sugar. We also heated up some chocolate sauce and drizzled that on
top, too.

![finished cakes](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOVGX4LVWsnWDHNLmxsP38jgzLR8qrMBsBuxyY1L9Vy5sHKw4ZI87sb7vmnLxWFILiswJtRmhaqoWjx1T1b6vbOXnM209aKYoNpeRJwQJfgMUPyyysUnYHNUVp9fGm8Knypmc1OsXmhUvvAWmkqjQxc=w883-h593-s-no-gm?w=500)

The cakes were delicious. Light, fluffy, and crispy with a hint of sweetness. They reminded us
of being at a carnival. And making them was extremely fun, too! I'll definitely be pulling out
this recipe as a treat every so often.
