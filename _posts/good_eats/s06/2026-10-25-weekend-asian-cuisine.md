I can't believe I haven't made any Good Eats recipes in October so far. It's been busy, but
since I had a free Saturday I thought I'd do some Good Eats dinner. I've been saving the
{% good_eats_recipe_link Asian Slaw %} to pair with the {% good_eats_recipe_link Chimney Tuna Loin %},
so Saturday was when that could all come together.

First, though, Sunday night's dinner was the {% good_eats_recipe_link Garlic Shrimp Casserole %}, so
that's where we'll start. I have been putting this one off, since the episode depends on leftover
Chinese takeout, which is a challenge for us because finding gluten free takeout is near impossible
around us. But, by chance I checked altonbrown.com and it turns out that his [revised recipe](https://altonbrown.com/recipes/garlic-shrimp-casserole/)
doesn't rely on takeout at all -- it makes the garlic shrimp, too. So I first set out to make some
home-made leftovers.

## Making Garlic Shrimp Leftovers

I began by marinated some shrimp in olive oil and garlic.

![marinating shrimp](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMRqMx7sHIc-9WT4iwZT5XJQAK9gwbou9BEnNhYaQwdhjK3pOBylg7DP8JWjiVE8uWPeTf-kp3VLyrLJyMZhz_ONOWTHPW7BKop-fZrRd1G_OPhsUqjGxF5_0Ng93NpNREZuMMStjN7qsIx7Qqg2rzg=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

Then I cooked it in a cast iron skillet on medium heat.

![cooking shrimp](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczP7xuz120mM8_oA1InGxxepH6U7rVZfTzRmNdoW0Lw1txkmc_ldUZKdvGQ92mBrZ_uDmqnt3qClWE0kWKqVjabFjI25q6dOyct_poOBnSlsd-_3fMqUpyiqt4mPHQyUVpAX63mMO0PtaH7AiN5qhXye=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

Once the shrimp was done, it was time for the vegetables. For this I used a frozen pack of stir-fry
veggies.

![stir fry veggies](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOZmNH-uYb-xZJ8BkHI-uqZ85t1RkFjTVVjqrcKW__sboDLmrzWPuU4U_U4Bhdqln7mxO2LLJa4WhF8G--ry_m4LcbGwDaJRRXSrb3vTssjFHsy5b0aqkio4YVe2I4XOewRPEGuIcqySIVLtN_V-6ZG=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

I decided that one the shrimp was done I'd just re-use the already hot pan that had some garlic and oil
in it. I just dumped the frozen veggies right in.

![stir fry veggies cooking](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOSccChD4uNhRC7hlnzY0YJkznrx1gelxhL8SwpPDJUzLNL3RKBuwP6H6zvRLhj351Kwr9-2C8VZVvcWCCZpum4W6oTVDa5JIuFoRYe6pRqLLRWdk7vUmXamD7yqd-WNVJqyfCeNYxnUQRrskmcXvhM=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

Meanwhile I cooked up some jasmine rice. When the rice and the veggies were done, I tossed them all together
in a bowl, and voila, I had garlic shrimp, veggies, and rice leftovers. I popped the bowl in the fridge to
wait for Sunday's casserole.

![instant leftovers](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczP_9AxFDrP0BF3a4r5MkYo-Sel25sP74w4og79MbCF-dRER6rcbpmZNq0Y7yHDYgHDGcpb6sxVOhOV0u_80hQSztd1CwJ7DiNKihq_5xXNqF0vyjv8af_hsP7nnUABoY79WFr6aFLXiJKLPTd5J1sEv=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

## Asian Slaw

Once the leftovers were ready, I turned my attention to the Asian slaw. I started with the dressing.

![dressing ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNki069lqOabWoO-yS2NsLqTQnzJh14ratwWD4EF6CLEuuNxupPRn6fXFVNFY-4ZNYnRnNy_BFC9iRXZIsDRVYJjJbwzfDO5EsJp7klZdjvSWGtRQKMGKcEof-s6dpQiGeYRc8hHZEZRSVxHd7Cos-K=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

All I did was toss them all together in a large bowl and whish until it was all mixed. After that I gathered
the rest of the slaw ingredients.

![slaw ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMfkNtSA2IYrO4GoTd0y31l6bMmOsdb83PZ1bbpfoYk0d-6NFKbVfbndQCQ7JyW5gFfo3fCwNRcOu9McLWC5MsruQiCtgIZsAV4PB0nc3s66QID0tBoBAV2lURBBa0_nd0EtdC6kyWFl0r5EOGOTTT6=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

One substitution I made: rather than serrano chiles, which I couldn't find, I used a large pablano pepper. I
think it didn't make much of a difference in the end. Once all of the ingredients were chopped, I mixed them
all together into a large bowl.

![asian slaw](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOQ2Hf3q95Tx_-ThcgLrL8LziYqXlvuEViyC0RfooCaVV4PBdd3aepvJRa4BxknJcO-LHU3z8BfLR0N9HQMUGwkV_nFu-wXOe5kDyJeau4XAo4ln9YG4Ja9_cZgeLNmyJnu-oc7jxqzxocFJN0k-dJ1=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

The bowl went into the fridge for a few hours while I made the tuna. For this I started with an absolutely
beautiful 2-pound piece of yellowfin.

![hunk of tuna](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMgvLt1zuxLGjL4rIvOHGzyPxYfrk22fht_SRNKJYsAyvsPSZyaxfx2CVK2C9mYcK7FpzCTSdyT3A7irqwPiCp6V-Rm7hC24YYMGOVNQN5aylD8fP1wxwzGIPpU2Xu3vPGZmQGbe1jZrwIP6QSSKLBj=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

Like in the episode, I trimmed it so it was somewhat rectangular and then cut it into two one-pound pieces.

![trimmed and cut tuna](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPifr1kGoa3QEBJB4nhg6IRdKH9DlcNYLhbi5X9Y9MskeQXFHxtRHQFd_3WzrR4OFuN9MUN3zukx1tkoBxVvbnnrGn0jXKNs0yk3vPxyzvh9qd-RIOEEZZjCATRIfcMHDM0C7h-M_WPJ2BJ5Z1b4nDs=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

I mixed up the simple marinade and put it in a container with the tuna for about two hours. The episode and
the online recipe say to use wasabi power, but I didn't think the family would enjoy that, so instead I used
a few teaspoons of ginger powder.

While the tuna was marinating I got my chimney starter prepped. I decided to put the starter on my little
smoker, and use a grate from my propane grill to cook on.

![prepped chimney starter](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNQqS_uSf9dqCpuljPie3i8LzfLCAvkRu_ShTKigrFdKOEFeWvXpVmwVAI7Et1O_9il9GwYhHfm1QfkgG0t0kfVix3C_TgHLr3o62seNjcgZRcQKHwTBXyoahBABIMCRuztQvBGn06GFNEGCW8wBiDA=w2230-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

When I was ready to start cooking, I got the chimney start lit.

![lit chimney](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPx-Akm5iKQme2zvy6JchBCfjCqdB96Thx6q121KbEEtOYSQft5bXl__l0kwr6vEtrfx-Ep51eHTm2usr40leJYhivgtvnZgcg6C_cQ6OHUousgmw5QnLPebnkKgjfdFbhYs2y5IrYn_lRrjahb5NkG=w1254-h1672-s-no-gm?w=400)

It was then a matter of time to wait for the coals to burn out and get super hot. While that was happening, I
got some sushi rice on the stove. It took maybe 30-40 minutes for the chimney starter to get ready to cook
on -- before that, the flames were going way above the top of the chimney. But once it was ready, I then
coated the cuts in sesame seeds.

![sesame seeding the tuna](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPn2tQAOyFYQUkuhJ3-v6ne3R2dUtrEaiJ7AyQugDhEE15dcquvcqFUGf7BD1Gd86ycBaMtPkQqx0dWyON4hPe-SFpXsq67scIrBibCdVMuS6kXMQxZ1MOJGvuDagaAfcZzhUVfEsikTzOuMjkt_xwQ=w1254-h1672-s-no-gm?w=400)

It wasn't as pretty as the episode, but it worked. I then brought them out to the coals and one at a time I
cooked them, 30 seconds per side, for a total of two minutes per cut.

![cooking on the chimney](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczP_0mMjkSa6wz57gcvZolTbN4zJXLhylKgE7FVuF1e3Dy13jQe6usP9xL_r3GFDNgB0t23rJAElOIUU-g-J0aCJ8DnmorJMhGB3T391NuFSoLSuNmefNJTlWsQswKAkoYgicqZvUYcAIScLgqZ8howB=w1128-h1672-s-no-gm?w=400)

After each piece came off the "grill" I put them on a plate with tented foil. I then brought it all inside,
and let them sit for a couple of minutes before cutting into them. When I did, they were just perfect.

![cut tuna](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOS6oznbSYP-kNay6MDOTOghgllttm9j7PWi-y2Ax7GsgA1dnFQUNWpt7H0e2LFPfbkrDZVIHLz-Ryp9hv7CO1Ek2Xcb72jzv0cynw3LaXO5eodi-lOEc_EObOJI0b5cnthzmpBTFWoyEpSzQ3cSF5Q=w1254-h1672-s-no-gm?w=400)

OK, maybe I could have done more than 30s per side to get a sear line around the edge, but that didn't matter
at all. We served the tuna in bowls over rice with some of the slaw on the side, and some of the honey-soy
marinade as a topping/dipping sauce, and I have to say it all worked together perfectly. The tuna was as good
as a perfect filet, and the rest of the meal complimented it incredibly well. What a treat!

## Shrimp Casserole

On Sunday night, it was time to put together the shrimp casserole. With the manufactured leftovers, it all
went together pretty quickly. I simmered some chicken broth, added a corn starch slurry and then some heavy
cream, and put it all together with the leftovers mix in a 9x13 baking dish.

![prepped shrimp casserole](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMrzJQaP9Hytg_SBuezVhh4HMCPv-05kF3zfyGXDVVUDAXFwSVXRk00uwbLEzJEEcOzaF-2xIc6zfXH1-j2I9EgLYAWitMC7DYjwkBem_32Hg7BY-6c8e817dsvJgBSDnb9vp4UupZ-fRipWt3TmAmn=w2414-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

After covering the top with some breadcrumbs I popped it in the over for about ~25m, then gave it a few
minutes under the broiler to toast up the top.

![finished casserole](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNl1ZJ1GQKquh4LOi8m3ItTX2KRASWaFbe6juA1bQYRhm2uKV8JwToiTPhwmV9ozYYz0NoAvII1tw3TqDABrF8jAzeklZOe-JK6RblBTsGv-y2gubhfIzEkT2en51My405r7lJITcKeSnwe4cUYULu8=w2462-h1672-s-no-gm?w=500)

We let it sit for a bit to cool, and the dug in. It was alright. I liked it enough to have seconds, and Vivian
devoured it. Gemma thought it was a bit bland and mushy. I can completely see why using actual Chinese takeout
leftovers would do the trick here -- they use so much flavor, sauces, salt, etc. that the shrimp and veggies
would be way better. Here I was going for at least a little reasonable healthiness. Another thing I might do
differently if I ever do this again would be to double up on the veggies, and use less of the liquid binder.
I think those two things together would reduce the mushiness.
