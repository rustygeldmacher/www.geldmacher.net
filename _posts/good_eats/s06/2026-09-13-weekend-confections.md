Some people get really excited about making sweets and sugary stuff. I must have been born without
a sweet tooth, because I am definitely not one of those people. So, I thought since I
had a free Saturday that I'd try and knock out the remaining season six sweets. My plan was to
use the {% good_eats_recipe_link Gold Cake %} to make a batch a cupcakes, and then frost them using
{% good_eats_recipe_link Cocoa Whipped Cream %} for half, and {% good_eats_recipe_link Buttercream %}
for the other half.

Now, the first stop as always was the comment section of the recipes. From what I gathered, the gold
cake recipe was not that great, and the buttercream even worse -- most commentors said it completely
didn't work. A little more research, though, turned up something interesting. Turns out that Alton
himself recognized this and redid both of these recipes in his Reloaded follow-up series. So, I
decided I'd used the [reloaded gold cake](https://altonbrown.com/recipes/reloaded-gold-cake/) recipe
(called "A Far, Far Better Cake") and the [reloaded buttercream](https://altonbrown.com/recipes/five-ingredient-buttercream-reloaded/)
recipe as well.

Of course the cake would need to be gluten free, so I turned to [Claude](https://claude.ai) to try
and work out how to modify the recipe. From this, I learned that the recipe _should_ be good by just
substituting [Better Batter](https://www.betterbatter.org/products/gluten-free-all-purpose-flour/1%20lb)
gluten free all purpose flour and making no other modifications. So, I gathered up my ingredients.

![cake ingredients](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPawT03u3nrUyuKxX_dYrZ1Mn35ceM_j6jI1r-iXwu8csxc6FE4iUnqxdyBluB1SrIQ5hTYufmFuQ0Iah_hc4FaId7r29ge3UzflByh8qWUT735Fegxxgown2WmqpUFcizVHx1aWJEgDdOgd3vwZYEI=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I halved the recipe because I only wanted to make a dozen cupcakes. That calculated out to 1 stick of
butter, 170g sugar, 120g flour, 7g baking powder, 1/8 tsp salt, 4 egg yolks, 150g milk, and 1/2 tsp of
vanilla. The batter came together easy, just like the recipe.

![cake batter](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOBr2KdfUkE8gawDW39kuoDZZDVbBYUAp9vv52dvarWrCDl2I63PKhgg6Y69sxfDAwXjZVAvLfdL4jhSMf0DGvY83maCGSpHaNLCmycYDJeYlONE5k8aPNZQ0uyR5wN4NrQ1OhWj-vyXtdXxxa2Rpnd=w2316-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

The one change for GF is to rest the batter -- I let it just sit there for 25min or so after mixing. I
then used a #20 scoop (3 tablespoons) to portion it out into 12 cupcakes.

![portioned batter](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNln8p76FrcQg1XlN64YYGiEYOvGE0iarNB0WT0rAfeoz1IWsI9u7_yaPWQQZtnoWFu97CdubwuU8s5tzW8kw34zmwXSnur7NP5gRUYQB2nHwJWf_3VgbQ05DMq9KVohmIrX_OJCl7ylhTXGmzCK8WT=w2596-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

Turns out there was just exactly enough for 12 cupcakes. I then popped them in the oven at 350 and set
a timer to check them after 16 minutes. After 16 minutes I checked and they were very close -- temp was
about 200 degrees (the recipe advises you to check) -- so I gave them another 2 minutes and they were
perfect after that. First I let them cool in the pan for 5min and then I took them out and put them on
a rack to cool. Meanwhile, I cleaned my mixing bowl and popped it in the freezer while I did went out
and did some errands.

When I was back I turned my focus to the cocoa whipped cream. I first made a (half) batch of cocoa mix.

![cocoa mix](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOeDZ8P1UrmNhWrY_btxtiia_7hcUKcL2Jsg_p9Z-tvOrEvhx3jJoubAWXGebKbtdTn_AAvKIrSHwHtcQu7_BJoJBVye-RrxRgxMTSTMEKpCeQ-310U0nrQn2yoPXp-mHFRHcepPJDDg-K79Zmgl1cb=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

This one went together pretty darn easily. I followed the recipe exactly, and ended up with really nice
whipped cream with medium peaks.

![medium peaks](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNIEFEkNRKZcA1C2f_Og07DgybdZiubpOCrkrGBwhI6CtCBkd0kukgsXb-TS0hEWuEGDF8FdGQDfQI85f5GEapMyYK-CuRPTOiymzRUUvhO-4-dGHXU57Ke3khzTxob2pwZIvyeyutSPLYWg0Yk6JfW=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

After this was done I switched my attention to the buttercream. Just like the cake, the buttercream has
a reloaded recipe that changes things quite a bit. And like the cake recipe, I halved this one, too. I
used a whole egg and then added enough whites (left over from the cake) to get to about 110g. Everything
else scaled down by half quite nicely. I began by mixing the eggs, sugar and corn syrup.

![eggs and sugars](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOcZMUAYxCFbyPbELvqxJtz28cszkT8dwsZNpb-guKpRvHcRqcrODfCKVRZyT9R2eM5mxf6i0bdzyeM2jGsFloHosno6j5bpMYN3koOriTHVEE9BLm2ZXCUTATgyRnwmHmdjbK4laTiXdmVy37CnR8M=w1572-h1780-s-no-gm?w=400)

And then I popped that over a dutch oven with water and a tea towel. Then, I whisked it non-stop for the
next ten minutes. The mixture became whitish in color and the sugars were definitely very well dissolved.

![stirring in the hot water](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNQXfxmjwWkb8YK9UdtPQceMKY_gPiJCwCCCo7HJcPbEeWGQMve1mlMt2P-KAeJoa-W2-OYeM_yfFPy0Pf4lWNAR4Ffcniv4E5yD7H3SQW-YTgNx7729T7GIp3A0tdjooWomlw_ucGAtAJVjdsRxLC2=w1598-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I measured it about 150, short of the target, but I was getting tired of whisking so I took it off the
stove and moved the bowl to the mixer. There, I let the mixer run for a long time -- I think it took about
15 minutes to get the temperature down below 80, when I started adding pats of butter. The butter at first
didn't incorporate so well, but as I added more, it all started coming together and eventually -- almost
suddenly -- I had something approaching frosting.

![buttercream](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNEQ-QcEB1_gd0_OwHmS4nNtOWlWgUK_REnchRDcdyed9aOlyDXcRHb5EuJ1ybeOPycjCXkeo52gK2j9_WzP_DmpGhzHb-edgExy83ZqfLQbt3apqQzVHGOYbRH7Na33wsBKbSYpzeTLA_TP7BViysh=w2374-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

As a slight deviation from the recipe, I added a half teaspoon of vanilla right towards the end. Once the
buttercream was done, I filled a piping bag with it and frosted half of the cupcakes. I then grabbed the
cocoa whipped cream and put that into a piping bag, using it to frost the other half of the cupcakes.

![frosted cupcakes](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNTzxvmEhv3lg7wRC3uQs0TuR08EVy0SbRva4KdRpMLoa9f6U6q4RM7bXGD-i_5IR57X55hX7fShsmh34cMktGJS7-rTqCm3sJlTXn2TotryZesjwuuX2EASfhp-NLzn-vX2Vj6wgswgSyerDUgHQGD=w2696-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

It was finally time to try them. I got the girls and we all grabbed one for a taste. The cake itself was
_really_ good. Light and fluffy, moist, and super flavorful. A great cake, even for gluten free. The cocoa
whipped cream didn't do a great job as frosting, but the buttercream -- as painful as it was to make --
was seriously delicious. And why wouldn't it be? It's basically just butter and sugar.

On Sunday night, I made a little dessert using strawberries and {% good_eats_recipe_link Writing Chocolate %}
along with the rest of the cocoa whipped cream. Preparing the chocolate couldn't have been easier: it's just
microwaving chocolate chips combined with a little canola oil. Once again I halved the recipe, and used a
half cup of semi-sweet chips and a teaspoon of oil. I microwaved it for a minute, stirred, then another 30-45
seconds. It didn't come out of the microwave looking melted but I stirred it until it was smooth and melty. I
then prepped a piping bag to spoon it into.

![melted chocolate](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczO7m58NgqOYRSg_22KI_jz2_aqHFQ8AmJLy83gDJZkCN_zx7Q8YK5nuKZREynOyP0hqBu6ND9_xIL6QNjc1gvI4V1fvM-6l8RKLpnhnqjWPhOZcFknyR6sEUA2UYRXDwHiJ3r-kP6TQWEichXpwY6IS=w2694-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

From there I piped a bit onto a couple plates, put strawberries and the cocoa whipped on it, and then piped a
bunch more over everything.

![strawberries and chocolate](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczO3msxH0MhA-j0P24tzt-_nd9hTDAisLJf0vc3pAESw0m2xxeVVWNyhmZRZUd0tS1go8hp7Qq2CHnHfwoTChw9WcxmXw-3FHgoUczPxP0akz8pag8VczHEyCtWZVfwazM6ZdrYCnX6gpAnm3MzerFOb=w2464-h1780-s-no-gm?w=500)

I thought it came out looking pretty good. The whole thing was delicious -- how could you go wrong with
melted chocolate? Also, the chocolate in the piping bag stayed workable for hours.
