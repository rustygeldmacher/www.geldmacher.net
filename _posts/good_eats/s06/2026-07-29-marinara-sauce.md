Tomatoes are coming into peak season around here, so I went out and bought 20 fresh romas
to try out some {% good_eats_recipe_link Tomato Sauce %} from scratch.

![tomatoes](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPamXSiNDY2EGzhAE18f_AttIqn1HoPERo3NtqXCDt8khfhB8GO-PjW3r4OliUuKf-4skOKrwRpZbih8nwZaIzqLxmuh1l_m1v2kOHeVb59NMhs15r5QkC-Nkr7Wahd9GIHfqDjLUVBmw8FUvlxh0V3=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

I started by splitting and seeding them all, then putting them in some glass baking pans. After that
I drizzled them all with some olive oil (I didn't bother measuring), then added the chopped garlic
and then the onion, salt, and pepper. I was using dried herbs so I decided to save them for later --
the thought being that dried herbs would just burn with 2.5hr in the oven.

![ready to cook](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPxfPP6MGnRWofOp_lxbMpOdOyVMSNFx1Ita9piX0gvf_UrWQbwBTqs2DXAb29JfQBvJ22WIqE23NZkVUJUrVtVoqQC-fCWt9OeKhV5ob1obCGiG1vsj44CDny3swDOThfy1VjsEr8ZYAJroGKgizVC=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

Before I did this recipe I read through the comments on the Food Network site. This one is odd: there
are plenty of 5 star ratings and plenty of one star ratings. The one star ratings almost all say that
everything came out of the oven burned to a crisp. So, following some of the five star comments I
cooked them at 300 degrees for the first hour, 325 for the next half hour, and then I did the last
half hour at 400 like the recipe says.

![cooked tomatoes](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPxG8MUc1imzULA8_aFcZTmvAyymK9DQflLDiXpC-WJxexT5F53iIhq7tsSmb1A2aLJ46AAV3pPpzgss4JvkBaJD3eDQJVjgUaUjeqgCFVdvIVa1OuCkbunKT14LQCrdi7FepUGeOw15wqLudWJX6FQ=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

I think I got it just right -- cooked perfectly with just the right amount of caramelization. Though,
the bottom of the pans was _caked_, which took a lot of elbow grease to clean. One commenter said he
used parchment paper on the bottoms of the pans, so were I to do this again I'd try that. After the
tomatoes were done it was time for the food mill.

![in the food mill](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNcAslkD3vlT2QQnXKlZWmgCiyQipm8SMWuYxI1vmV3k7_ZDQ5_torlvqVqWhn5YJrinzijL9JnQLbAz3Xs9e3yIQ2WQyp6BXVigTMlU51T-HB64C_jG5dTc2MckSz8qcKfX7PovIXIeZ1yQtalfJIx=w2410-h1808-s-no-gm?w=500)

This was the first time I used a food mill, so it was a bit of a learning experience. I think I did
it right, and after 10 minutes or so of cranking I had a mill full of skins and about a cup and
a quarter worth of sauce.

![sauce](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczM5G_9LdmE35rMFHLxNh9mvC9DkIoQtegfTz4cYkLKT8t6WmUTByLdsO6UgbYbXiOi0FgVa4uuk0PK1Lsuj6kalGalCOVZWoDxi0AeJik9O3kYMb-e3ea7oeAtf4IR53JWFhrdXcY1FvI_u0CEbelbG=w1356-h1808-s-no-gm?w=400)

Lots of the comments pointed this out -- for all the tomatoes and all the work put into the recipe,
you really don't end up with a lot of sauce. I just don't know how in the world the recipe gets to
the 4 cups as listed on the site.

Regardless, I put that resulting sauce in a pan and added some wine. The recipe says to add a full
cup of wine, but I figured since I only got a little more than a cup that I'd only add a quarter
cup of wine. Also, I added the herbs at this point, too. I let the sauce come to a boil and then
simmered it for a while before I tasted it. On the first taste, it was _really_ bitter. I had read
in the comments that this could happen and the solution was to add sugar. I added a little bit at
a time, and after about a teaspoon worth, it started tasting pretty good. I heated up some left over
pasta and topped it with the sauce.

![pasta with sauce](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMjbN-dEare-_9E6n06cKqZSnKoYzVseeLJUzP1N5y1AMe1l2mRq1Z0NgQq10aC1-KkER3glsPauEmJSgSLm2ePFeGZjiFvAalQtX9srz-xjceLdD0zppJB__8YyjOPZWOhMYWb0TmcWIC4-hkrpDJt=w1356-h1808-s-no-gm?w=400)

The sauce definitely has the consistency of a marinara -- the kind of sauce you'd get with some
mozzarella sticks from the local pizza house. I am not sure I'd really do this one again based on
the amount of work you need to put in for the result, but my family really liked it, so it was all
worth it.
